As AI tools have progressed, there is a temptation to say that “productivity should increase by n number of times”. While CEOs like to make this claim and are excited about the future of these tools, I am a little bit skeptical of claims like this. The underlying assumption of these claims is that the bottleneck in a feature or product is engineering time. While every company, product, and feature are different, in my experience this is a rare occurrence.

It’s not that AI will not increase productivity as it undoubtedly already has, but claiming that because the coding part is now faster, we are widening *the* bottleneck is simply false. It has increased my productivity by a guesstimate of 20–30% as I spend less time looking up solutions. I have, many times, largely finished the coding parts of my work for the change to be held up by Product, QA, or managment. This is a natural part of iterative design and while coding itself has never been easier, getting the right solution remains something one must intuit based on experience.

As AI grows in popularity, I wondered how senior developers with a ton of knowledge would be able to use AI and leverage their experience. I have recently begun to use AI heavily to essentially replace web searching for solutions to a problem or syntax error. However, I still find myself looking through my personal Wiki I have on my machine with solutions to common problems or gotchas in iOS development. I have kept this folder of markdown files for a long time so that I can easily load them into VS Code and search for a solution. This approach has served me well and I think eventually I will be able to tie these two things together as we approach a future of on-device models. I look forward to seeing how much faster I can actually accomplish tasks with all the other constraints we have as a team as these tools evolve.
